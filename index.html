Hi, I'm Rojin!

Some people know me as Nazdim, and either name is great.

I'm a Biostatistician II in social epidemiology at the Stanford Center for Population Health Sciences (PHS), where I work with Dr. David Rehkopf. My work is a collaboration between PHS and the Stanford Center on Poverty and Inequality (CPI), with Dr. David Grusky. I'm also pursuing an MS in Computer Science at Georgetown University.

My work focuses on predicting population health behaviour, with the goal of improving health outcomes. I combine LLM-powered social simulations with classical NLP and machine learning, working with electronic health records (EHR) and long-form narrative data. It builds on my work on the limits of LLM-simulated societies, which showed that alignment can erase real cross-cultural differences.

More broadly, my research sits between computer science and health. I study how large language models behave in medical settings, from cognitive bias to how they respond when patients arrive with an incorrect self-diagnosis, and I've helped build AgentClinic, a benchmark for evaluating AI in simulated clinical environments. I'm also interested in causal inference, speech and language technology for Persian and other low-resource languages, and what LLMs can and can't tell us about human societies.

Before this, I was a software engineer on Capital One's AI Foundations team, and a research assistant at Stanford with Susan Athey, at Johns Hopkins with Anjalie Field, and at the University of Maryland, where I earned my BS in Information Science and was the department's 2023 commencement speaker.

Research

A few threads that run through my work.

Current focus
🧑‍🤝‍🧑

Social simulation for public health

Predicting population health behaviour with LLM-powered social simulations, alongside NLP and machine learning on EHR and long-form narrative data.

🩺

AI in medicine

Evaluating medical language models: cognitive and implicit bias, susceptibility to patient self-diagnosis, and agent benchmarks like AgentClinic.

🌍

Language and culture

How LLM alignment flattens cross-cultural differences, plus speech recognition and NLP for Persian and other low-resource languages.

⚖️

Causal inference and fairness

Using generative models and causal methods to study bias in hiring and in micro-loan decisions.

🗳️

Computational social science

Modeling how elections and political events shape public attention and knowledge production on Wikipedia.
